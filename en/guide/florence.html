Florence: Cradle of the Renaissance & Tuscan Handbook

Where to stay, Uffizi Gallery booking hacks, luggage storage near Santa Maria Novella, and wine windows.

🏨 Where to Stay in Florence (Best Areas)

Florence is a remarkably compact, highly walkable open-air museum:

  • Centro Storico (Duomo & Signoria): Steps away from world-class masterpieces, historic squares, and artisan shops. Perfect for first-timers.
  • Oltrarno & Santo Spirito: Across the Arno River. Bohemian, artsy vibe filled with local antique restorers, craft workshops, and excellent authentic trattorias.
  • Santa Maria Novella Station Area: Highly practical for train travelers arriving from Rome or Venice, offering quick transit access.

🚆 Arriving in Florence: Santa Maria Novella & Peretola Airport

High-speed trains from Rome and Milan arrive directly at Firenze Santa Maria Novella (SMN) station right on the edge of the historic center. From Florence Peretola Airport (FLR), the T2 tramway reaches downtown Florence in just 20 minutes for €1.50.

🏛️ Uffizi Gallery, Accademia & Brunelleschi's Dome

The Uffizi Gallery (home to Botticelli’s Birth of Venus), Michelangelo’s **David** at the Accademia Gallery, and climbing **Brunelleschi’s Dome** all sell out weeks ahead. Pre-booking timed skip-the-line entrance tickets is essential to avoid hours in line.

💡 4 Practical Tips Foreigners Must Know in Florence

  • Historic Wine Windows (Buchette del Vino): Look out for small architectural stone arched windows built into historic palazzos where historic noble families once sold flasks of Chianti directly to passersby. Today, many are active again serving wine, coffee, or gelato!
  • Bisteca alla Fiorentina: Authentic Florentine T-bone steak is traditionally served rare (al sangue) and priced by weight (usually sold for 2 people or more).
  • Piazzale Michelangelo Sunset: Walk up or take a bus to Piazzale Michelangelo overlooking the city for the most breathtaking panoramic sunset view across the Florentine skyline.
  • Tap Water is Drinkable: Florence features public drinking fountains (fiaschette) supplying fresh spring water throughout the city center.
